Thumbnails

Seconds between preview frames.
seconds
Heads up: at 10 seconds, some files may take longer to process.

Videos already contain "snapshot" frames — complete pictures stored at intervals. When those snapshots are closer together than your interval, we just reuse them — fast. When they're spaced further apart than your interval, we fully decode the video to generate a unique frame for each slot — correct, but slower for that file.

We check every file automatically and pick the right path. The slower path mainly hits streaming downloads (web releases), which often have 4–9 second gaps between snapshots. TV episodes and Blu-ray rips usually have snapshots every 1–2 seconds and stay fast at any interval.

Shorter intervals give finer scrubbing in your player — the tradeoff is that some files take longer to process. Both choices are fine.

How it decides

Every file goes through the same four steps.

  1. 1
    Ask the sources, in your order
    The list under Where we check. Once a type is settled by anything other than chapters alone or season audio alone, the rest are skipped as “not needed”. Your servers' own markers are always read.
  2. 2
    Check who agrees
    Two answers agree when intro ends are within 5 s or credits starts within 10 s. When sources agree, the one higher in your order supplies the times.
  3. 3
    Decide
    A marker is sent when a chapter isn't contradicted, when two independent sources agree, or when a source that reads the file answers. Otherwise nothing is written for that type; you can add or adjust one in the Inspector.
  4. 4
    Send to your servers
    Each server gets the decided markers. With Keep Plex's or Keep Emby's on, that server's own marker of a type stays, unless it's impossible for the file (outside it, or under 3 s).
